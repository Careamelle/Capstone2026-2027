Chapter 1

The Problem & Its Setting

Abstract

This capstone project presents a frugal engineering framework for a decentralized pipeline leak detection system utilizing a raw LoRa star topology. Aimed at mitigating Non-Revenue Water (NRW) in municipal utilities—specifically modeled for Barangay Cabid-an, Sorsogon City—this architecture bypasses the prohibitive costs and recurring SaaS fees associated with commercial LoRaWAN gateways.

The edge node integrates a Heltec WiFi LoRa 32 V3 (ESP32-S3), a 5V analog piezoresistive pressure transducer, and a highly efficient MT3608 power-gating circuit, keeping the hardware footprint strictly under a ₱2,000 budget per node. Telemetry is transmitted over 915 MHz to a central receiver, pushing data to a Firebase backend. A Flutter-based micro-GIS dashboard processes differential pressure algorithms to successfully triangulate active leak zones while filtering out systemic false positives, such as scheduled municipal pump shutdowns.

Background of the Study

Water scarcity and resource mismanagement remain critical challenges globally. Locally, in areas like Sorsogon City, specifically Barangay Cabid-an, Non-Revenue Water (NRW)—water that is pumped and then lost to leaks before reaching the customer—creates massive operational deficits for Local Government Units (LGUs) and water districts.

Currently, municipal leak detection relies heavily on manual acoustic sounding or reactive reporting (waiting for a pipe to burst visibly). While advanced commercial IoT systems exist, they are heavily centralized. They depend on expensive LoRaWAN gateway infrastructure, cellular backhauls, and subscription-based cloud servers. This financial barrier prevents smaller, rural municipalities from modernizing their water grids. By applying "frugal engineering," this project seeks to deliver enterprise-grade telemetry using low-cost, off-the-shelf components without relying on third-party network providers.

Statement of the Problem

This study aims to develop a cost-effective, decentralized pipeline leak detection system. Specifically, it seeks to answer the following technical challenges:

  • 1. How can a reliable, battery-operated pressure-sensing edge node be designed and constructed for under ₱2,000 using frugal engineering principles?
  • 2. How can a raw 915 MHz LoRa star topology be effectively implemented to ensure reliable data telemetry while bypassing commercial LoRaWAN gateway dependencies?
  • 3. How can a backend differential algorithm accurately distinguish between isolated pipeline degradation (true leaks) and system-wide pressure losses (e.g., scheduled pump shutdowns)?

Objectives of the Study

General Objective

To design, prototype, and validate a frugal, decentralized pipeline leak detection framework utilizing a raw LoRa star topology for municipal water monitoring applications.

Specific Objectives

  • To construct an edge node utilizing a Heltec ESP32-S3, an analog piezoresistive pressure transducer, and a custom P-Channel/N-Channel MOSFET power-gating circuit to maximize battery life.
  • To deploy a centralized receiver node that bridges raw LoRa packets directly to a Firebase real-time database.
  • To develop a Flutter-based mobile Micro-GIS interface equipped with a differential pressure algorithm to visually map the node statuses.

Significance of the Study

This project holds significant practical and academic value for various sectors:

For Local Government Units (Sorsogon City)

Provides a viable, low-barrier entry point to modernizing water infrastructure. Reducing Non-Revenue Water directly correlates to massive financial savings and better resource conservation without crippling budget allocations.

For Utility Maintenance Crews

Eliminates the guesswork in locating leaks. The micro-GIS Flutter app allows workers to go directly to isolated zones, saving man-hours and reducing the time roads need to be excavated for exploration.

For Future ECE Researchers

Establishes a proven foundation for "Frugal Engineering" in IoT. It demonstrates how to successfully bypass commercial SaaS constraints by utilizing raw protocols, opening doors for advanced research into mesh topologies or machine learning integration on the edge.

Scope and Limitations

This capstone project is strictly scoped as a bench-tested proof-of-concept prototype. While the system architecture and algorithms are meticulously designed and calibrated for the physical and geographical context of Barangay Cabid-an, Sorsogon City, the final output will not undergo live, underground LGU utility deployment due to bureaucratic and timeline constraints.

Financially, the edge node design is strictly capped at a ₱2,000 maximum budget to validate the frugal engineering framework. Technologically, the network topology completely excludes the use of commercial LoRaWAN gateways (such as The Things Network), relying entirely on a raw 915 MHz LoRa configuration developed directly between the edge nodes and the centralized Firebase receiver.
